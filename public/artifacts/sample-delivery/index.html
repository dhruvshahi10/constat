What was done

Of the 24 questions in acme_security_questionnaire.xlsx, 17 were answered from Acme's own approved evidence. Each of those answers carries the document, version and location it came from — 19 citations in total — so a buyer's security team can check any answer against the source rather than take it on trust.

7 questions were refused. The engine found nothing it was permitted to cite, so it wrote nothing. That is the control working, not a shortfall: an unsupported answer on a security questionnaire is a statement Acme would have to stand behind later, and the engine is built so it cannot produce one.

Those 7 refusals are your work-list, set out in evidence_gaps.md. Each one names the document that has to be produced, renewed or reconciled and who it was routed to. Close them and the same questions answer themselves from evidence next time.

3 answers are cited and clean but still await a named reviewer's sign-off. They are marked as such in the working paper; they are not represented as approved.

Your trust page answers 13 of the 38 questions buyers most commonly ask, without anyone at Acme being asked. The remaining 25 are shown as open items a buyer must request, because publishing an answer that is not fully evidence-backed is how a trust page starts lying.

The commitment register checks 7 promises already made in contracts and RFP responses against the same evidence. 7 of them cannot currently be stood behind.

The audit log covering this engagement verifies intact. Every step — receipt of your questionnaire, classification, drafting, each gate decision, every approval and the final export — is recorded and hash-chained. The log is tamper-evident: an edit to any past line breaks the chain from that point on. It is not signed, so it evidences that history was not edited, not that it could not have been rewritten from scratch.
